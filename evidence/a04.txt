A04 — Before and after the clock change
Evidence type: Phone measurement summary
Source: Ashen_Movement_Timing_Finding_2026-10-19.txt
Selected source lines: 97-127



Before, v0.1.4-diagnostic:
- 934 retained completed-frame samples; 438 (46.9%) had zero elapsed ticks.
- Remaining samples had two ticks at a reported rate of 64 Hz.
- All 92 retained guest key events matched the overlapping Java event log.
- Neutral movement commands were zero while position continued changing.
- One uninterrupted example was still moving 1.194 seconds after release.
- Median observed completed-frame callback gap: 16.647 ms.

After, v0.1.5-clock-trial:
- 2,816 retained completed-frame samples across 61.757 seconds.
- One zero-time sample: the first captured frame, stationary at startup.
- All 2,815 subsequent captured samples had positive elapsed time.
- Tick deltas: 2,621 samples of one tick, 192 of two ticks, two of three ticks.
- All 120 retained Java dispatches matched guest input events.
- Median observed completed-frame callback gap: 16.678 ms.
- Seven clean high-speed grounded releases reached zero horizontal velocity
  in 321.710-342.649 ms (median 326.537 ms).
- Their velocities decreased monotonically and positions stayed fixed after
  stopping. Two of the neutral intervals lasted 7.484 and 8.616 seconds.
- Floor-contact state remained set through those braking sequences.
- The user's gameplay video and assessment agreed with the measured repair.

The seven release examples require: at least one second before the next
movement press, zero movement commands throughout, initial raw horizontal
speed >=45000, continuous floor contact through stopping, and the first
sample within 50 ms of the guest release. Overlapping inputs, short gaps,
airborne releases and already wall-stopped inputs are excluded. Positions
and velocities are original fixed-point integers; no metres conversion is
assumed. The histories are sampled/retained traces, not every event forever.
