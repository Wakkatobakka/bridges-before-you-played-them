DoJa / Development history

First came the silence.

We had a Japanese game running in complete silence. Getting the rest back meant building on an English patch, protecting checkpoints, trying different ways to aim, and finding several ways for audio to fail.

The first report has 3,425 submitted frames, 54,599 draw calls, and no sound. We had Dirge running. Playing it comfortably on my phone would take more work than getting those first frames onto the screen.

First footing

At 0.1.0, we could reach VincentApplication.start and submit thousands of frames. The text was Japanese, the report said “silent diagnostic mode,” and progression saving was still an open question. Those were the next things waiting for us.D01

In 0.1.1, we integrated the verified English 1.0 package, with the patch credited to TurquoiseHammer / Yuvi. Their work gave us an English version to build on.D02

Then we needed to protect progress. The 0.1.2 notes keep the original game’s checkpoint system and record local fresh-process Continue testing. A room reached in one session needed to remain reachable after closing the bridge. The exact phone close/reopen sequence still needed testing, but we were following the game’s checkpoints rather than inventing save states.D03

The wrong kind of success

The internal 0.1.6 build finally decodes an MLD. It turns 166,032 bytes into 663,160 PCM samples at 8 kHz. That sounds like the audio breakthrough—until the very next line.D06

Two consecutive lines / internal 0.1.6 D06
AUDIO DECODE  Decoded 166032 byte MLD to 663160 PCM samples @ 8000 Hz
AUDIO ERROR   play failed: IOException: AudioTrack initialization failed

The decoder had done its job. Android’s output track had refused to initialize. “Audio enabled” would have been an impressively unhelpful way to describe that result.

The next recorded output path uses a 48 kHz mono streaming AudioTrack. The decoded samples are resampled, queued and started. In 0.1.7, the log finally carries both the start and the completion of the stream. The useful difference is down in the events: the sound has made it through the backend instead of stopping at a successful decode.D07

Then the short sounds find a different way to be awkward. A stream that works for a long track does not automatically finish a tiny effect. In 0.2.0, a 3,048-frame sound is padded with silence to a stream length of 11,520 frames; that selected effect now starts and completes. The original audible portion stays intact.D10

By 0.2.1, the log follows active audio into pause and back out again. Host pause, audio pause, host resume, audio resume: another small chain that needed to work before the recovered sound could simply belong to the game.D11

Meanwhile, the other thumb had opinions

We changed our minds about the controls more than once. Version 0.1.4 has a horizontal camera rocker. Version 0.1.5 tries a contextual camera/aim pad. By 0.1.8, we were back to compact four-way movement with horizontal aim. Each layout was a different attempt at making the game work under my thumbs.D04D05D08

In 0.1.9, the right-thumb trace takes a particularly useful turn: it forwards the original RIGHT and LEFT D-pad aim inputs. The game gets to do its own aiming through a more comfortable touch surface.D09

There is still a wonderfully irritating bug left for the later preservation notes to explain. Compile-only camera stubs carried stale pad constants, and the compiler baked those numbers into the touch-input code. The running camera used different numbers. A right-side touch could wind up meaning left—or vanish into the mismatch.D14

The 0.2.4 repair changes those constants into runtime field reads and drops the failed Action-to-Aim pointer handoff. Action and aim become separate controls again. A problem that looked like a bad touch idea had a very specific extra cause hiding in the compiled interface.D14

Getting the bridge out of the way

The 0.3.0 pass brings in the shared gameplay UI: the original 240×320 view, a square sliding D-pad, separate horizontal aim, a prominent Action button and an inline original keypad. The workbench can change around the recovered game without rebuilding its working runtime; the handoff checks 662 retained methods after packaging.D12

The later handoff records my confirmation that 0.3.0 worked. I then asked to get the entrance dashboard out of the way and open straight into gameplay when the data was present. Imports and the checkpoint vault moved into utilities. The 0.3.1 record keeps 414 selected gameplay/UI methods from the build I tested while narrowing the change to that routing.D13

We’d started by trying to get Dirge to execute. By this point, I was asking the bridge to get out of the way so I could play it. The saved handoff ends with that new direct-launch flow still waiting for its phone check.D13

About these records

This chapter follows 14 report exports and the later preservation handoffs through 0.3.1. Two exports extend one shared session; they are retained without counting two independent runs. Backend audio events do not replace listening evidence. One separately retrieved Dirge-labeled report invokes Rockman code, so it stays outside this failure timeline. Exact export dates are not consistently established. The receipts preserve those distinctions without interrupting each turn of the story.D15D16

D14 / The 0.2.4 aim repair: a mismatch in compiled constantsCheckpoint verification notes

Source: Dirge v0.2.4 / VERIFICATION-SUMMARY-v0.2.4.txt · selected lines 3–21, 32–40

The note states that physical-device execution of 0.2.4 had not yet been performed.

Scope
- Bounded Weapon Aim repair only.
- Removed the failed v0.2.3 Action -> Weapon Aim pointer handoff.
- Weapon Aim / Field Camera is again an independent touch control.
- Baseline APK: exact v0.2.3 device-tested build.
- Only TwinInput and TwinInput$Contact are replaced in classes.dex.

Root cause found
- The compile-only CameraAssist stub used stale synthetic pad IDs:
  PAD_LEFT=23, PAD_RIGHT=24 (and the rest shifted similarly).
- The actual runtime CameraAssist uses PAD_LEFT=24, PAD_RIGHT=26, PAD_UP=25, PAD_DOWN=27.
- Because the old stub exposed those IDs as static-final constants, javac inlined the stale numbers into TwinInput. The right side of Weapon Aim could therefore be interpreted by the runtime as LEFT, while another direction could be ignored.
- v0.2.4 makes the compile-only PAD_* fields non-final runtime reads and aligns their documented values with the runtime.
- javap verification confirms TwinInput bytecode uses getstatic CameraAssist.PAD_LEFT and getstatic CameraAssist.PAD_RIGHT rather than inlined integer constants.

Control behavior
- Action remains independent.
- Weapon Aim / Field Camera remains horizontal-only.
- In first-person, the Weapon Aim strip feeds the same native left/right aim bridge already proven by the game.
